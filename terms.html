What Whereabouts is

Whereabouts is an iPhone app and a service run by its developer (contact below). The app maps rooms, keeps a list of where things are, and guides a person who is blind or has low vision to them by voice. The service keeps your account and your rooms, shares rooms between phones, and sends photos to Claude, an AI service run by Anthropic, to name what's in them and read labels. The privacy policy says exactly what's kept and who handles it.

What's free, what minutes are, and what Plus is

Mapping minutes are how mapping is paid for after that: each minute bought gives your account one more minute of mapping a room with Claude (and looking inside drawers and closets), with the same allowance of photos and Claude's answers as the free minute. Minutes are bought as you need them, in any number, don't expire, and are your account's (and, like the free minute, your email's: an account deleted and made again with the same email keeps nothing).

Whereabouts Plus is a subscription for Find: it lets Claude find things the phone can't see and read labels, on your phone and in every room you share. One subscription covers the rooms you share with your family. Plus doesn't include mapping minutes.

Safety: what Whereabouts is and isn't

Whereabouts is a memory and a guide, not a mobility aid. It works alongside a white cane or a guide dog, never instead of them. It warns about what the camera sees, and it can miss things: a low step, a glass door, something at head height, a thing that moved since the room was scanned. On an iPhone without LiDAR it sees much less of what's in the way. You stay responsible for how you move and for your own safety, and you should use it at a pace at which your cane or dog can still do its job.

Claude can make mistakes when naming things and reading labels. Don't rely on a label read by the app alone for medicine, allergens or anything where a mistake could hurt you; check with a person or the original packaging.

Using it fairly
