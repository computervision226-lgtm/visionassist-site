Spatial memory for your things

Point your iPhone around a room and it tells you what's where, even inside a closed drawer. Made for people who are blind or have low vision, and for the families who help them.

Get it on your iPhone 17. VisionAssist is in open testing through Apple's TestFlight: install the free TestFlight app from the App Store, then open the link below on the phone and tap Install. The App Store release follows.

Join on TestFlight How to start

What it does

  • Scan a room (iPhone 17 Pro) in one walk-through: the phone measures the walls, doors and furniture, spots everyday things as you go, and Claude names everything in the best photos. Open a drawer or a closet and tap Look inside, and every spoon and shirt in it is listed too, pinned where it lies.
  • Ask for anything by voice: "Where are my keys?" You hear the answer at once, then turn-by-turn directions around the furniture and through doorways, with a warning before anything in the way, and in the last half meter your hand is guided to the thing itself.
  • Keep it current from a computer. Each room opens as a floor plan and in 3D in a web page, where the family renames things, adds notes, fixes the plan, and shares rooms with the person who finds things in them. When something can't be found, the family hears about it.
  • In English and Arabic, screens, directions and voice commands alike, and with VoiceOver.

What it costs

The app is free, and so is your first minute of mapping. Mapping more rooms with Claude, and Claude finding things and reading labels, need VisionAssist Plus, a subscription that one person's family shares: the price is shown in the app. Finding your way with the maps you have is always free.

Good to know

  • Use works alongside a white cane or a guide dog, not instead of them. It warns about what the camera sees, and it can miss things.
  • Your rooms and photos are yours: see the privacy policy for exactly what's kept and who handles it, and how to delete it all from inside the app.
